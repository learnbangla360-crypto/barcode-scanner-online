Step-by-step: how to scan a barcode

Expert tips and common mistakes

  • Keep the quiet zone. The blank margin on each side of a barcode is part of it. Cropping it off often causes a failed scan.
  • Keep bars sharp. Thin 1D bars blur easily. A sharp screenshot beats a shaky photo.
  • Hold it straight. A barcode tilted or curved around a bottle is harder to read. Flatten the label if you can.
  • Beat the glare. Glossy packaging reflects light. Move the code until the shine slides off the bars.
  • Don't over-compress. Heavy JPEG compression smears bars together. If you need a smaller file, use Compress Image File at a high quality setting.
  • Check digits matter. EAN and UPC codes end with a check digit. A damaged code may not read at all rather than read wrong.
  • The number isn't the product. A barcode holds a number only. Product details come from a database, which this tool doesn't look up.

Troubleshooting

"No barcode found" message

Use a sharper, larger image where the whole barcode and its white margins are visible. Crop away clutter, or try a screenshot instead of a photo of a screen.

The camera won't start

Allow camera access in your browser's site settings. Close other apps using the camera. The page also needs HTTPS.

The barcode is on a curved or shiny package

Move the code until glare disappears, or photograph it from straight on. Flat labels read far better than curved ones.

The scan reads a wrong number

Rescan at higher quality. A blurry image can be misread as a different valid code. Compare the result with the digits printed under the bars.

My file won't open

Some formats, like HEIC, may not open in every browser. Convert the photo with our HEIC to JPG Converter, then try again.

The library couldn't load

Check your connection, disable any blocker that stops jsDelivr, and click Scan barcode again.

Nothing found in a photo with several barcodes

Crop so only the barcode you want is visible, then scan that smaller image.

Glossary

  • Symbology: a barcode type and its encoding rules, such as Code 128 or EAN-13.
  • 1D barcode: a code made of vertical bars of different widths, read across one line.
  • 2D barcode: a code that stores data in a grid, like QR or Data Matrix.
  • Check digit: a final digit calculated from the others to catch misreads.
  • Quiet zone: the blank margin on each side of a barcode.
  • ISBN: the book number often printed as an EAN-13 barcode starting with 978 or 979. See Wikipedia's ISBN page.

Barcode scanner FAQ

Is this barcode scanner really free?

Yes. This online barcode scanner is free to use with no trial, paywall or hidden limit. You can scan as many codes as you like from images or your camera. You don't need an account, an email address or any installed software to use it.

Do I need to sign up or download an app?

No. Open the page in a modern browser, drop in an image or click Use camera, and scan. There's no registration and no app to install. It works on Windows, macOS, Linux, Android and iOS wherever your browser allows file selection and camera access.

Is it safe to scan barcodes here?

Scanning only reads the number or text inside the code. This tool doesn't open links or send the value anywhere. Your image is processed in your browser. If a QR code holds a web address, copy it and check the domain before visiting it.

Are my images uploaded to a server?

No. The image is decoded inside your browser and isn't uploaded. Camera frames are processed on your device and aren't saved. The only download is the ZXing reader library, which loads from a CDN the first time you scan. Your pictures never leave your device.

Can I scan a barcode from a photo or screenshot?

Yes. Save or screenshot the page, then drop the image into the box. Make sure the full barcode and its white margins are visible and sharp. A screenshot is more reliable than a photo of a screen, because it avoids glare and moiré patterns.

Which barcode types can it read?

It reads EAN-13, EAN-8, UPC-A, UPC-E, Code 128, Code 39, Code 93, Codabar, ITF, QR Code, Data Matrix, PDF417 and Aztec. It reads one code per scan and shows the format beside the value. Other, less common symbologies aren't supported.

Does this barcode reader work on mobile?

Yes. On phones and tablets you can pick a photo from your gallery or use the camera. Your browser asks for camera permission the first time. Hold the phone steady, keep the barcode flat and well lit, and it usually reads within a moment.

Does it work offline?

Not on first use. The reader library has to download once from the CDN when you click Scan barcode or Use camera. After it has loaded, scanning in the same session needs no further network requests. You need a connection to open the page itself.

Can it look up the product behind a barcode?

No. A barcode stores a number, not product details. This tool shows you that number and its format. To find the product, paste the number into a store, a manufacturer site or a product database. For book barcodes, the number is usually the ISBN.

Why does it say no barcode was found?

The image may be blurry, too small, cropped too tightly or covered by glare. Use a sharp image with the entire barcode and its white margins visible. Also confirm it's one of the supported formats, and try cropping the image so the barcode fills more of the frame.
